BMI (Body Mass Index) is a simple ratio of weight to height squared. It's not a perfect health indicator, but it's the most widely used screening tool because it requires no equipment beyond a scale and a tape measure.

## The Formula

**Metric:**
```
BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m)
```

**Imperial:**
```
BMI = (weight (lbs) × 703) ÷ height² (inches)
```

## Worked Example

A person weighing 70 kg at 1.75 m tall:

```
BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75)
BMI = 70 ÷ 3.0625
BMI = 22.9
```

## BMI Categories (WHO Standard)

| BMI | Category |
|---|---|
| Below 18.5 | Underweight |
| 18.5 – 24.9 | Normal weight |
| 25.0 – 29.9 | Overweight |
| 30.0 – 34.9 | Obese (Class I) |
| 35.0 – 39.9 | Obese (Class II) |
| 40.0 and above | Obese (Class III) |

## Where BMI Falls Short

BMI doesn't distinguish between fat and muscle. A highly muscular athlete can have a BMI in the "overweight" range while having very low body fat. Conversely, someone with a "normal" BMI can have high visceral fat.

**Better alternatives when precision matters:**
- Body fat percentage (DEXA scan, calipers)
- Waist-to-height ratio
- Waist circumference alone

BMI is a useful starting point for population-level screening — not a personal fitness verdict.
